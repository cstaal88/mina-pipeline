## Resolved

- **A 40-hour silent outage.** The archive hit an undocumented 40 MB write ceiling
  and every upload was refused, unnoticed, for two days. Fixed — the archive now
  rolls monthly with size and shrink guards, so it cannot recur.
- **Monitoring.** `python3 getnews/health.py` reports health on demand; an automatic
  check every two hours opens a GitHub issue on failure and closes it on recovery.
  The alarm was tested by deliberately tripping it.
- **Under-collection alarm** *(new)*. Two August days had been collected at 2% of
  normal and went unnoticed for two weeks. There is now a check that compares each
  day against the trailing median. Verified against the real data: it flags exactly
  those two days and nothing else — the quietest normal day runs at 41% of median
  against a 25% threshold.
- **Filter defects.** 443 irrelevant records left the broad tier — most matched only
  because "White House" contains the keyword `house`. The focused tier was
  unchanged, record for record.
- **Recovered data.** 117 RSS records from the outage (including primary election
  night) and 261 MediaCloud records from the two under-collected days.

## Decision: the MediaCloud backfill

**The question.** The corpus starts in early July. Earlier MediaCloud data exists in
the archive but was collected under the old Minneapolis-ICE query, so it is not
midterms coverage. Backfilling 1 Jan – 30 Jun would add roughly **24,000 records**
at the observed ~135/day.

**What it costs in MediaCloud credits.** Measured today rather than estimated: two
single-day re-fetches moved weekly usage from 128 to 160, i.e. **16 API calls per
collection-day**, one per source.

| | Calls |
|---|---|
| Full backfill, 181 days × 16 | 2,896 |
| Ongoing daily collection | ~448/week |
| **Weekly limit** | **4,000** |

The entire backfill fits inside a single week's quota at ~84% utilisation, and
comfortably inside two. Current usage is 160 of 4,000 (96% remaining).

**Credits are not a problem.** Two other things are:

1. **Storage — must be fixed first.** The backfill adds ~22 MB to a 21.6 MB file
   against the same ~42 MB write ceiling that caused the August outage. It would
   break the pipeline the same way. Sharding the MediaCloud archive is roughly a
   day of engineering and is a hard prerequisite.
2. **Throughput — the real limit.** Fetching runs ~26s per source-day and article
   scraping ~25 URLs/min, so a full backfill is on the order of **16 hours of
   processing** against a 90-minute job cap. It has to be chunked: about 9 runs with
   a raised timeout, or ~36 at current settings.

**Bottom line:** feasible and affordable, but it is roughly a day of engineering
plus a few days of supervised chunked runs — not a button press. If we skip it, the
corpus begins 10 July and we say so in the methods.

## For the methods section

- The RSS corpus begins **10 July 2026**. Earlier RSS was destroyed by a bug and is
  unrecoverable — RSS cannot be backfilled, as feeds carry only current items.
- ~1,150 RSS records were lost on 11–12 August during the outage; 117 recovered.
- MediaCloud records before July were collected under a different query and should
  not be read as midterms coverage.
- August 2026 is split across two archive files. Everything reads both; this affects
  citation, not analysis.

## Recommendation

Decide the backfill question on scientific grounds — the engineering is tractable
either way. Beyond that, stop: the pipeline is in good shape and further tuning has
reached diminishing returns.
